# Floras Social Post Skill
**Format:** Social post / LinkedIn caption / short-form content
**Reads after:** `SKILL.md` → `style-guide.md` → this file

---

## Purpose

This file governs the generation of Floras social content. The primary platform is LinkedIn — where Floras' enterprise buyers, suppliers, and sustainability-adjacent professional audience lives. Posts may also be adapted for other text-based platforms, but LinkedIn is the default and the tone benchmark.

The goal of a Floras social post is not to go viral. It is to build a consistent, credible presence among the people Floras needs to reach — procurement leads, sustainability directors, supply chain professionals, and climate-focused investors. Every post should leave the reader with either a new understanding or a reason to follow.

A well-written Floras social post reads like something a knowledgeable person said — not like a brand announcement.

---

Before generating any social post, confirm all of the following. If any are missing, mark `[NEEDS INPUT]` — never invent them.

| Input | Why it's needed |
|-------|----------------|
| **Post type** | What kind of post is this? See Post Types below. |
| **Core idea or topic** | What is the post about? One clear idea only. |
| **Any stat, fact, or proof point to anchor the post** | Social posts without a concrete anchor feel vague. If none is available, mark `[NEEDS PROOF]`. |
| **Platform** | LinkedIn (default) or other? Affects length and tone. |

## Post Types

Every Floras social post falls into one of five types. Identify the type from the brief before generating. The type determines structure, length, and hook strategy.

### Mechanism Post
Explains how Floras works or how one specific part of it works. Written for someone who has heard of Floras but doesn't fully understand it. Goal: clarity. Not a product ad — a plain-language explanation.

### Insight Post
Shares a perspective, observation, or data point about supply chain emissions, corporate sustainability, or the decarbonization space. Floras may or may not be mentioned. Goal: build credibility and attract the right audience. This is the highest-value post type for organic reach.

### Proof Post
Highlights a verified outcome, project, partner, or milestone. A specific number, a named partner, a certificate issued. Goal: demonstrate that Floras delivers real-world results. Never vague. Never invented.

### Founder Voice Post
Written in the voice of Gonzalo or Jeroen. Personal, first-person, reflective. Can share a behind-the-scenes perspective, a lesson learned, or a conviction about the space. Goal: humanise the company and build trust. Tone is warmer than other post types but still disciplined — not confessional, not performative.

### Announcement Post
A new partner, a new project, a new feature, a new milestone. Factual and specific. Goal: inform the audience of something that happened. Never hype. The fact does the work — the copy just frames it.

## Post Structure

Each post type has its own structure. Follow the correct one for the type identified in the brief.

---

### Mechanism Post Structure

**Hook (1 sentence)**
- State something about the mechanism that is surprising, counterintuitive, or clarifying.
- Do not open with "Floras is a..." — open with the idea, not the brand name.
- Example: "Every time a company receives goods from a supplier, a small climate action happens automatically."

**Explanation (3–5 sentences)**
- Walk through the mechanism plainly. One idea per sentence.
- Use the approved flow where relevant: transaction → Floras issued → allocated to verified project → impact certificate received.
- Do not introduce terminology not in the style guide.

**Anchor (1 sentence)**
- A concrete proof point, stat, or specific detail that makes the mechanism feel real.
- If none is available, mark `[NEEDS PROOF]`.

**Close (1 sentence)**
- A forward-motion statement or a question that invites reflection. Not a hard sales CTA.
- Example: "That's what supply chain decarbonization looks like when it's built into the transaction itself."

---

### Insight Post Structure

**Hook (1 sentence)**
- A sharp observation, a striking stat, or a question that makes the reader stop.
- Must be specific. "Supply chains account for over 80% of enterprise greenhouse gas emissions" is a hook. "Sustainability is more important than ever" is not.

**Development (2-3 sentences)**
- Expand on the observation. Add context, nuance, or a second data point.
- Floras may be mentioned here as a mechanism that addresses the insight — but it is optional. The insight stands on its own.
- One idea per paragraph. Never combine multiple distinct points into a single paragraph block — each new idea gets its own paragraph with a blank line between them.

**Implication (1–2 sentences)**
- What does this mean for the reader's world? Make it concrete and relevant to the audience.

**Close (1 sentence)**
- A question, a provocation, or a forward-motion statement. Invites engagement without begging for it.
- Example: "The question isn't whether supply chains need to change — it's whether the tools exist to make that change measurable."

---

### Proof Post Structure

**The fact (1 sentence)**
- Lead with the specific result, partner, or milestone. No preamble.
- Example: "Floras is now live with Puro Earth — one of the most rigorous carbon removal registries in the world."
- If the fact is unverified, mark `[NEEDS PROOF]` — never publish an invented proof point.

**Context (2–3 sentences)**
- Why does this matter? What does it mean in practice for Floras users?

**What it means for readers (1–2 sentences)**
- Connect the proof point to the reader's context — what can they now do, trust, or expect?

**Close (1 sentence)**
- A forward-motion statement. Not "stay tuned." Something specific.

---

### Founder Voice Post Structure

**Opening (1–2 sentences)**
- Personal, specific, grounded in a real observation or moment. First person.
- Not: "I've been thinking a lot about sustainability lately." Too vague.
- Yes: "Last week I spoke with a procurement director who told me they'd been trying to measure their supplier emissions for three years." Specific moment, real context.

**The reflection (3–5 sentences)**
- What did this make you think? What does it reveal about the space, the problem, or the opportunity?
- Can reference Floras, but doesn't have to. The point is the insight, not the product.

**The conviction (1–2 sentences)**
- A clear, stated belief. Not a question — a position.
- Example: "Supply chain decarbonization doesn't need more reporting frameworks. It needs a mechanism that makes the right action automatic."

**Close (optional, 1 sentence)**
- A question to the audience, or nothing. Founder voice posts can end on the conviction.

---

### Announcement Post Structure

**The announcement (1 sentence)**
- State the news directly. No build-up, no "we're excited to share."
- Example: "Floras has joined Berkeley SkyDeck's accelerator program."
- If the announcement fact cannot be verified, mark `[NEEDS PROOF]` — never publish an unverified claim.

**Why it matters (2–3 sentences)**
- Context that makes the announcement meaningful. What does this enable? Who does it affect?

**Forward motion (1 sentence)**
- What happens next, or what this makes possible.

## Formatting Rules

Floras social posts are plain text. No markdown, no headers, no bullet points in the post body itself. Line breaks are used for rhythm, not structure.

- **Length by platform:**
  - LinkedIn: 150–250 words for most post types. Founder voice posts may run to 300 words if the content warrants it. Never exceed 300 words. Announcement posts are an exception — 80–150 words is appropriate and often stronger.
  - Other platforms: adapt to platform norms — this file does not govern non-LinkedIn formats in detail.
- **Line breaks:** Use a single blank line between paragraphs. Do not use double line breaks or visual separators.
- **Hashtags:** 3–5 maximum on LinkedIn. Place at the end of the post, never inline. Use specific, relevant tags — `#supplychainemissions`, `#decarbonization`, `#sustainableprocurement`. Never generic tags like `#sustainability` or `#climatechange` alone.
- **Emoji:** Not used in Floras social posts. The tone is professional and restrained. Emoji undermine the quiet confidence voice.
- **Tagging:** Tag named partners or people only when the post is directly about them and the tag is warranted. Never tag people speculatively for reach.
- **No "we're excited to announce" or equivalent.** The fact announces itself. State it directly.

---

## Tone Notes for Social Posts

Social is the most public Floras artifact — it is visible to anyone, read without context, and judged in seconds. The tone rules from the style guide apply with these additional constraints:

- **One idea per post.** Posts that try to say three things say nothing. Identify the single point before writing.
- **The hook is everything.** LinkedIn truncates after the first two lines. If the first sentence doesn't earn a "see more," the post fails. Write the hook last, after you know what the post is actually saying.
- **Never lecture.** Floras cares about the climate — but the audience already knows the stakes. Posts that explain why climate change is bad will be scrolled past. Posts that offer a specific mechanism or insight will not.
- **Founder voice is personal, not confessional.** Vulnerability is not the goal. Credibility and authenticity are. Share what you've observed and what you believe — not what you've struggled with.
- **Proof posts live and die by specificity.** "We're making an impact" means nothing. "310 kg CO₂e removed per Flora allocated" means something. Always name the number, the partner, or the project.

## Consistency Check (Social Post-Specific)

Run these in addition to the five questions in `SKILL.md` Step 6:

1. Is the post type correctly identified and is the right structure being used?
2. Does the first sentence work as a standalone hook — specific, not generic?
3. Is there exactly one idea in the post?
4. Is the post within the correct word count for its type and platform?
5. Are hashtags placed at the end, limited to 3–5, and specific rather than generic?
6. Is the post free of emoji, "we're excited to announce," and any banned phrases from the style guide?

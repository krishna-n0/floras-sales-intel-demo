# Floras Email Skill
**Format:** Sales email / outreach / follow-up
**Reads after:** `SKILL.md` → `style-guide.md` → this file

---

## Purpose

This file governs the generation of Floras sales emails. These are written communications sent by Gonzalo, Jeroen, or the Floras team to enterprise buyers or suppliers — for cold outreach, warm follow-up, post-meeting summaries, or re-engagement. The goal of every email is not to impress — it is to earn one specific response: a reply, a booked call, or a confirmed next step.

A well-written Floras email is short, specific, and easy to act on. It reads like it was written by a person, not a system. It does not try to close a deal — it tries to open a conversation.

## Email Types

Every Floras email falls into one of four types. The type determines the structure. Identify it from the brief before generating.

### Cold Outreach
First contact. The recipient has no prior relationship with Floras. The goal is to earn a reply — not to explain everything. Keep it short. Lead with relevance, not with Floras.

### Warm Follow-Up
The recipient has had some prior contact — a conference, a referral, a downloaded resource, a previous email with no reply. Open by referencing the connection. Do not pretend the prior contact didn't happen.

### Post-Meeting
Sent within 24 hours of a call or meeting. Summarises what was discussed, confirms any commitments made, and states the agreed next step. This is a record as much as a communication.

### Re-Engagement
Sent to a contact who went cold after initial interest. Acknowledge the gap without dwelling on it. Offer something new — a project update, a relevant development, a changed context — as a reason to re-open the conversation.

## Email Structure

Each email type has its own structure. Follow the correct one for the type identified in the brief.

---

### Cold Outreach Structure

**Subject line**
- Specific, not clever. Reference the recipient's company or role.
- Max 8 words. No exclamation marks. No all-caps words.
- Examples: "Supply chain emissions — a question for [Company]" / "Floras + [Company]: worth 15 minutes?"
- Never: "Exciting opportunity," "Quick question," "Following up," or any subject line that could apply to any recipient.

**Opening line**
- One sentence. Specific to this recipient. Reference their company, their sector, or something relevant happening in their space.
- Never open with "I hope this email finds you well" or any equivalent.
- Never open with a Floras introduction. The recipient doesn't care about Floras yet — they care about their own context.
- The opening line must reference the recipient's company name or role explicitly. If no company name is provided in the brief, use `[NEEDS INPUT: company name]` — never write an opener that could apply to any recipient.

**The hook (1–2 sentences)**
- State the problem or opportunity that makes this email relevant to them specifically.
- Use approved language from the style guide. No banned phrases.
- If no specific hook or context was provided in the brief, mark `[NEEDS INPUT]` here — do not substitute a general industry trend or vague claim. A general trend is not a specific hook.

**What Floras does (2–3 sentences)**
- One short, specific description of the mechanism. Not a product tour.
- Example: "Floras embeds a reward currency into your supplier transactions. Every purchase generates Floras — currency you allocate to verified CO₂ reduction projects. You receive a compliance-ready impact certificate as proof."
- Never describe Floras as an offset marketplace, a CSR tool, or a sustainability platform.

**The ask (1 sentence)**
- One specific, low-friction action. A 15-minute call is appropriate for cold outreach. A yes/no question works well.
- Never ask for a long meeting, a demo, or a commitment at this stage.
- Examples: "Would a 15-minute call next week be worth your time?" / "Is supply chain emissions reporting on your agenda for this quarter?"

**Sign-off**
- Sender name, role, and one contact detail (email or LinkedIn).
- No inspirational quotes. No "Best regards, the Floras team." Always a named person.

---

### Warm Follow-Up Structure

**Subject line**
- Reference the prior connection directly.
- Examples: "Following our conversation at [Event]" / "Re: our conversation at [Event]"
- Max 10 words.

**Opening line**
- Reference the prior contact in the first sentence. Be specific — name the event, the person who made the introduction, or the resource they engaged with.

**Brief recap (1–2 sentences)**
- Remind them of the relevant context without repeating everything. One sentence on the mechanism if they haven't been fully briefed.

**New or additional value (1–2 sentences)**
- Give them a reason to engage now that wasn't in the prior contact. A relevant project, a stat, a development in their sector.
- If nothing new exists, mark `[NEEDS INPUT]` — do not pad with filler.

**The ask (1 sentence)**
- Same rules as cold outreach. One specific, low-friction action.

**Sign-off**
- Same rules as cold outreach.

---

### Post-Meeting Structure

**Subject line**
- Always: "Following our conversation — [date or topic]"
- Never a generic "Great speaking with you."

**Opening line**
- One sentence. Thank them for their time without being effusive. "Thanks for the time on [day]" is enough.

**Summary (3–5 bullet points)**
- What was discussed. Factual, not interpretive.
- Each bullet is one sentence.
- If a commitment was made by either party, it appears here as its own bullet, prefixed: "Agreed:"
- If an open question was left unresolved, mark it: "Open: [question]"

**Next step (1–2 sentences)**
- The single agreed next action, with a date or timeline if one was set.
- If no next step was agreed, mark `[NEEDS INPUT]` — do not invent one.

**Sign-off**
- Same rules as cold outreach.

---

### Re-Engagement Structure

**Subject line**
- Acknowledge the gap implicitly without being self-deprecating.
- Examples: "Checking back in — [relevant context]" / "Floras update — relevant to [Company]?"

**Opening line**
- Acknowledge it's been a while. One sentence, no over-explanation.
- Example: "It's been a few months since we last spoke — I wanted to reach back out."

**The new hook (1–2 sentences)**
- Something has changed or a new development makes the conversation relevant again. Name it specifically.
- If nothing genuine has changed, mark `[NEEDS INPUT]` — never manufacture a reason.

**Brief Floras reminder (1–2 sentences)**
- A short re-introduction to the mechanism for recipients who may not remember the details.

**The ask (1 sentence)**
- Same rules as cold outreach. Low-friction, specific.

**Sign-off**
- Same rules as cold outreach.

## Formatting Rules

Floras emails are plain text first. They are not HTML newsletters. They do not have headers, dividers, or brand colors in the body. They look like they were written by a person.

- **Length:** Cold outreach and re-engagement: 150–200 words maximum. Warm follow-up: 150–250 words. Post-meeting: no hard limit, but summaries should be scannable in under 60 seconds.
- **Paragraphs:** Max 3 sentences each. One idea per paragraph.
- **No bullet points** in cold outreach, warm follow-up, or re-engagement — prose only. Bullet points are permitted only in post-meeting summaries.
- **No bold or italic text** in the email body — these read as shouting in plain text contexts and undermine the quiet confidence tone.
- **No attachments on first contact.** A link to a one-pager or deck is acceptable in warm follow-up or post-meeting emails only.
- **Sign-off:** Always a named person on their own line, followed by their role and one contact detail. Never "the Floras team" or any anonymous sign-off.
- **Font stack:** always declare `font-family: 'Rubik', -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, 'Segoe UI', sans-serif` in any HTML output. Never rely solely on Google Fonts.

## Tone Notes for Emails

Email is the most personal Floras artifact — it arrives in someone's inbox with their name on it. The tone rules from the style guide apply, with these additional constraints:

- **Write like a person, not a brand.** No taglines, no "Reward The Earth" in the body copy. Those belong on presentations and one-pagers.
- **Never oversell in an opening email.** The goal is a reply, not a conversion. One compelling hook beats a full product explanation.
- **Respect the recipient's time explicitly.** "15 minutes" is a concrete ask. "Some time to connect" is not.
- **Never manufacture urgency.** "Act before end of quarter" is not appropriate unless there is a genuine, named reason. If urgency is real, name the reason specifically.
- **No follow-up threats.** "I'll follow up if I don't hear back" belongs in a CRM, not in the email itself.
